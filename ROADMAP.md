# Roadmap

A hundred and fifty ideas, from next week to never, and the order to try them in.
Hard on purpose: most have not been done, some may not be possible.

## A hundred and fifty ideas

### Drivers that talk
1. An indicator output and a sense of other cars' indicators: does a signalling language evolve, and does it solve right of way where nothing else did?
2. A horn: an output others hear within a radius. Warning calls, or noise?
3. Eye contact: a car senses whether the car it sees is "looking" at it.
4. Measure the information carried by evolved signals (mutual information between signal and next action).
5. Dialects: do separate cities evolve different signalling conventions, and can a driver from one survive in the other?
6. Deception: does anyone evolve to signal one thing and do another, and do others learn to ignore them?
7. Turn-taking at a four-way stop with no lights, no rules, only signals.

### Cities that evolve too
8. Co-evolve traffic light timings with the drivers that obey (or don't) them.
9. A city planner genome: road edits proposed and kept when the economy moves more people.
10. Evolve speed limits per road.
11. Evolve where the taxi ranks are.
12. A city that grows where demand is unmet, by itself.
13. Braess hunting: evolution searching for the road whose closure helps most.
14. Evolve one-way assignments for a whole city (Barcelona's arrows, from scratch).
15. Roundabout vs light vs nothing, chosen per junction by evolution.
16. Pedestrian crossings placed by the pedestrians' own deaths.
17. A city of 10,000 cars on all of Manhattan, in a worker pool.

### Economics nobody has run

20. Two platforms competing for drivers.
21. Unions: drivers that refuse fares below a price, and whether that strategy spreads.
22. Insurance companies as agents, pricing premiums by observed driving.
23. Taxes that change with the vote of the drivers.
24. Inheritance: children start with a share of the parent's cash, or not.
25. Retirement, debt, credit.
26. Universal basic income for cars.
27. A minimum wage per fare.
28. Fleet owners vs owner-drivers.
29. Tipping that evolves on the passenger side.
30. Freight auctions and long-haul vs last-mile niches.

## Roadmap

Ordered by what is both new and doable in this code. One at a time, each
measured, written up, and kept or dropped.

1. **Signalling (1, 4, 7).** Add an indicator output and a sense of the
   nearest car's indicator. Raise populations from scratch with and without
   it; measure right of way, conflicts resolved, and mutual information
   between signal and action. The headline question: does talking solve
   what pricing could not?
2. **Phantom jams (52).** A ring road with uniform cars: do stop-and-go waves
   appear from nothing, and how do evolved drivers compare to an IDM model?
3. **Co-evolving lights (8).** Each light's offset and split as a genome, kept
   when the city moves more fares.
4. **Wardrop and price of anarchy (54, 55).** Compare evolved route choice to
   user equilibrium and to the system optimum on the real maps.
5. **Any city in the browser (65).** Type a place, fetch Overpass, convert in
   the page, drive it.
6. **Cultural transmission (36).** Children copy a rich car's route habits.
7. **Novelty search (32).** Behaviour descriptors from route, speed, side.
8. **City planner genome (9, 14).** Evolve one-way assignments for Barcelona.

Each wake-up picks the first unfinished item, moves it forward, tests,
commits and pushes.

## Done

- 34. Speciation by body niche (`ECON.speciesBy`): by body, by brain or both, no difference; Turin has no freight, so no body niche to keep apart
- 23. Taxes by vote (`ECON.vote`): drivers voting their pocket keep a basic income at zero; started at a quarter, two cities vote it away and two vote it to the 90% ceiling and collapse
- 126. Reviews (`ECON.reviews`): fares by the share of clean rides cut crashes 9% at no cost in fares, unlike shunning; the gap closes within a week
- 109. Fuel prices that follow demand (`ECON.fuelMarket`): no change in fares, crashes or Gini; the fleet swings a little less through the day
- 102. Insurance fraud (`ECON.payout`): a claim worth even a tenth more than the repair fills the city to its ceiling and stops evolution; crashers are not selected, nothing is
- 22. Insurers pricing by the record (`ECON.rated`): half the extra crashes of flat insurance, seven tenths of its extra fares
- 46. A living tree of life: each founder's family as stacked bands over time (docs/families.svg, tools/dynasty.mjs SVG=)
- 44. Evolutionary rescue: none after a quake; stopping mutation on day three carries 14-35% more fares, quake or not, mutational load (tools/quake.mjs FREEZE=1)
- 130. Dynasties: one founder's family is the whole city within 3-6 days, in three of four runs; the coalescence of population genetics (tools/dynasty.mjs)
- 21. Unions (`ECON.union`): refusing cheap fares dies out, within a day at a floor near the median fare (tools/unions.mjs)
- 130. Dynasties: one family owns the whole city by day 3-6 in three of four runs (two left on day 8 in the fourth); it wins by numbers, earning no more per car (0.26-0.85x the rest) (tools/dynasty.mjs)
- 150. The simulator explains itself: tools/explain.mjs, a plain-language account of any economy run from its telemetry
- Found on the way: the species threshold flipped every generation (step 0.3 as big as the threshold); step 0.02 settles it and raises fitness in 3 of 3 seeds, now the default for new populations
- 25. Credit (`ECON.credit`): an overdraft raises fares a fifth to a quarter, and crashes as much, in all four seeds
- 136. Migration: Turin's drivers fail in Manhattan every time; Manhattan's do as well as the locals in Turin (tools/migrate.mjs)
- 20. Two platforms (`ECON.platformB`): drivers settle at ecology's ideal free distribution, 59-62% against 61% predicted once few children switch (tools/platforms.mjs)
- 121. Licences (`ECON.licence`): a long test cuts crashes a tenth and fares a quarter to a third
- 132. Bankruptcy courts (`ECON.forgive`): a forgiven first bankruptcy changes fares and deaths not at all; crashes up a seventh
- 138. Tourism: a day of double demand doubles the fleet, then a wave of bankruptcies; back to normal within one to two days (tools/season.mjs)
- 45. Evolvability (in neat-from-scratch): the average mutant keeps 54% of its parent at 3 generations, 87% at 40; brains get sturdier
- 47. Mass extinction: cars recover in 3-6 hours; species radiate in one run in four, are lost in another (tools/extinction.mjs)
- 33. Open-endedness: two new species a day for 40 days but about 6 alive at a time, no upward trend; turnover, not growing diversity
- 110. Rush-hour toll (`ECON.peakToll`): between no charge and all-day, keeps more fares, still speeds trips
- 115. Snow: no caution evolves in a ten-day winter; crashes per distance 1.1-1.7x clear and the gap widens, fares recover instead (tools/snow.mjs)
- 48. Kin selection: none; relatives crash into each other slightly more than chance (tools/kin.mjs)
- 10. Speed limits per road: evolved cameras on 68 of Turin's 342 roads cost 14% of fares for 28% fewer crashes, about what as many random ones do (tools/limits.mjs)
- 13. Braess hunting (56): every closable road tried too, none saves more than 0.07%; closing any of the busiest 30-60 roads at the selfish equilibrium makes total time worse, by 0.65% at least, in all three cities; most cannot be closed without stranding a fare (tools/wardrop.mjs BRAESS)
- 8. One-way planner (9, 14): from no arrows, evolution makes a few dozen roads one-way and only under load; the real arrows cost 12-19% of fares in the sim, evolved ones 2-5% (tools/oneway.mjs)
- 4. Wardrop (54, 55): the price of anarchy is at most 1.6% on the real maps; shortest paths, as the drivers route, cost up to 2x at heavy load (tools/wardrop.mjs)
- 53. IDM fit: per brain, the IDM explains up to 22% with zero headway; what a driver sees explains up to 77%; mixed populations look like noise (tools/idm.mjs)
- 61. Self-organised criticality: no; jam sizes fall off exponentially, the largest 5 to 9 cars (tools/jams.mjs)
- 114. Earthquake: a tenth of the roads closed; Turin and Barcelona shrug it off, Manhattan loses 3-9% and more each day (tools/quake.mjs)
- 125. Reputation (`ECON.reputation`): shunning crash-prone cars does not lower crashes; crash counts carry no blame
- 118. Old drivers: a 0.2 s reaction delay quadruples crashes; evolved drivers depend on instant reflexes (DELAY=12)
- 117. Drunk drivers: noisy cars crash 3x as often; the city slows and the sober ones crash less (tools/drunk.mjs)
- 116. Fog: half sight makes drivers faster, not slower; crashes per distance unchanged (FOG=0.5)
- 27. Minimum fare (`ECON.minFare`): 15% more per fare turns into a few more cars, not more fares or equality
- 26. Basic income (`ECON.basic`): a quarter of fares shared lowers the Gini to 0.32 at no cost in fares, but trips take a quarter longer
- 24. Inheritance: a bigger start lowers the Gini (0.35 to 0.30) but costs 12-14% of fares; too small (5) and the economy never starts
- 129. Social mobility: earnings are not inherited (parent-child rank correlation 0.00-0.01); only the top two fifths breed (tools/mobility.mjs)
- 57. Critical junctions: closing the busiest roads first is no worse than random on real maps; the fragile ones are quiet one-way side streets

- 2. Phantom jams: none; stops are stationary (tools/ring.mjs, docs/ring.png)

- 60. Zipf: species rank-size slopes -1.0 to -2.0, too few species to say
- 59. Scaling: cars per road superlinear (1.25), fares 0.84 by construction
- 58. Percolation: real one-way cities lose half their connectivity at 5% of roads closed (tools/percolation.mjs)
